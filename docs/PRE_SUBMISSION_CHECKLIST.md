## Suggested framing for the draft

The evidence supports this, in this order:

1. A waveform-grouped 29-class benchmark across six noise conditions, with the
   naive-split inflation quantified (+8.18 pp) — a methodological
   contribution that stands on its own.
2. A hybrid that fuses a frozen learned time-frequency representation with
   handcrafted features and beats the strongest classical ensemble by
   **+2.44 pp macro-F1** (95% CI 1.06–3.81, *p* = 0.008).
3. **The mechanism**: the gain is entirely on the compound
   Sag/Swell + Harmonics + Flicker + OT disturbances, where handcrafted
   features saturate, at a small cost on the simple classes. This is the
   result to lead with.
4. An honest negative: at 10 dB and 0 dB nothing here beats the classical
   ensemble, and 10–11 of 29 classes are unusable at 0 dB. Reporting the floor
   is a contribution; claiming robustness that the data does not show is not.
5. Two-stage frozen training showed far lower run-to-run spread than joint
   training — reported descriptively, with the confound stated.

Avoid: "under severe noise" as a claim, "completely eliminates instability",
any per-class number quoted from `results/per_class/summary.json`, and the
seed-0 bootstrap presented as across-seed significance.
